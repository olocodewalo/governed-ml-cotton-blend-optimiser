# LinkedIn kit

## The plan

1. **Publish the article first** — paste `article_linkedin.txt`, cover image
   `slides/article_cover.png`. Copy its URL once published.
2. **Then the post** (Post A below), which carries the story and sends readers to the
   article.
3. **Then the comment thread**, posted by you, immediately.

Any URL not yet known appears as an `XXXX-...-LINK` token (article, repo). Fill them in
one command once you have them — the tokens themselves live in `set_links.py`:

```bash
python docs/linkedin/set_links.py --repo https://github.com/you/repo --article https://...
python docs/linkedin/to_linkedin.py     # regenerate the paste-ready article
python docs/linkedin/set_links.py --check   # exits 1 if any placeholder is left
```

## Which format, and what each one limits

| Format | Limit | Reach | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed post | **3,000 characters** | Highest — the feed favours native posts | The hook and the findings |
| Carousel / document post | 300 pages; PDF or multi-image | Highest, and holds attention longest | The 10 slides |
| **Article** | **~110,000 characters**, title 100 | Lower organic reach; found via profile, search and your own post | The full technical write-up |
| Newsletter | Same editor as an article | Article reach **plus** a notification to subscribers | Same content, if you have newsletters enabled |
| Comment | 1,250 characters each | Rides on the post | The depth, as a thread |

**LinkedIn News is editorial — a member cannot post into it.** The formats above are all
that is available to you.

**On links.** A link to your own LinkedIn article is on-platform and does not get
suppressed the way an external link does, so the article link belongs *in the post*. The
GitHub link is external — Post A keeps it because you asked for it, but if reach matters
more than a click, move that one line into the first comment.

**Paste-ready article:** `article_linkedin.txt`, generated by
`python docs/linkedin/to_linkedin.py` from [article.md](article.md). LinkedIn's editor has
no markdown, no tables and no code blocks, so that file has emphasis stripped, tables
flattened into readable lines, and `[H2]` / `[IMAGE: ...]` markers telling you where to
apply a heading style or upload a slide. Roughly 17,000 of 110,000 characters — length is
not a constraint.

Run `python docs/linkedin/check.py` after any edit: it verifies every block below still
fits LinkedIn's limits. **Hashtags and @mentions count toward the 3,000** — Post A is
2,583, so there is roughly 400 characters of headroom for them. Every number comes from `eval/baseline.json` (model v2, 50
held-out laydowns).

## Post A — the main post, drives traffic to the article

> My first write-up claimed a ~6% cost saving.
> I audited my own project and it fell to 3.0%.
>
> That audit taught me more than the build did, so I wrote up every technical choice and
> why. Short version here.
>
> **The decision.** In a ring-spinning mill, raw cotton is 55-65% of yarn cost. Every week
> a mixing master picks the bale laydown — which bales, in what proportion — trading ₹/kg
> against CSP, U%, imperfections and ends-down. In most mills that sits in one expert's
> head and a spreadsheet, isn't re-optimised when cotton prices move, and leaves no record
> of why a blend was chosen.
>
> **Why ML, not a rule.** Quality is non-linear in the blend. Short-fibre content penalises
> strength with an exponent above 1. Micronaire *variance* hurts evenness independently of
> the mean. Neps come from immature fibre and short fibre interacting.
>
> **What I built.** LightGBM for the four quality targets; P10-P90 bands calibrated with
> conformalized quantile regression; a MILP (PuLP/CBC) on a linear surrogate plus a DEAP
> genetic algorithm against the full non-linear model; RAG over 18 fibre-science notes with
> Claude writing the rationale — advisory only, it never touches a number; a Streamlit
> review screen, a SQLite audit log and CI.
>
> **Then I audited it against my own governance doc. Five gaps:**
>
> → The model failed its own policy — 67% and 68% interval coverage, against a 70-90%
> requirement I had written myself
> → An unapproved model was serving: training had silently overwritten the "current" pointer
> → Drift detection, an out-of-support check, a price-freshness guard and a contamination
> cap were all in the doc. None were in the code
> → The optimiser found that gap and put 54.6% of a blend into the one origin mills cap for
> contamination risk, because it was cheap
> → No CI — while the policy said the regression suite "fails CI"
>
> **Measured properly** on all 50 held-out laydowns instead of a 15-scenario sample: 3.0%
> saving at strictly matched quality (95% CI 0.8-5.5%), in-band 90%, false-accept 2%. And
> the finding I didn't want: 0 of 50 optimiser blends sit inside the model's training
> support.
>
> A smaller number I can defend beats a bigger one I can't.
>
> Full write-up — feature design, why LightGBM over a neural net, the conformal fix, MILP
> vs GA, the guards, why the LLM stays off the decision path, and the gate that refused my
> own model:
> https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-built-cotton-blend-optimiser-audited-until-headline-ankit-agrawal-qxecf/
>
> Code: https://github.com/olocodewalo/governed-ml-cotton-blend-optimiser
>
> Prototype on synthetic data — the point is the governance, not the number.
>
> If you run blending in a mill: what would you need to see before letting a system like
> this influence a real laydown?

## Comment thread — post these yourself, in order, right after publishing

**Comment 1 — the uncertainty fix, in detail**

> On the intervals, since that's the part with real teeth:
>
> LightGBM quantile heads trained on ~400 rows overfit in-sample, so the P10-P90 band comes
> out too narrow. My first attempt scaled the band by a calibration factor and still
> under-covered.
>
> The fix was 5-fold cross-conformalized quantile regression: train the quantile heads
> out-of-fold, compute conformity scores max(lo − y, y − hi), take the finite-sample
> corrected quantile, and widen additively. Under exchangeability that gives a
> distribution-free coverage guarantee.
>
> Coverage went to 0.76 / 0.80, inside the policy band — with no change to point accuracy.
> The predictions were never the problem; the honesty about them was.

**Comment 2 — why two optimisers**

> Why both a MILP and a GA, since people ask:
>
> The LP can't use the real model. Quality depends on weighted variances, which are
> quadratic in the blend weights — not expressible in a linear program. So it optimises an
> OLS surrogate fitted on the linear-in-w features, and then the full model re-scores the
> answer; if it fell out of band the safety margins inflate and it re-solves. The surrogate
> gap is exposed, not hidden.
>
> What the LP buys: 1-3 seconds, global optimum against that surrogate, deterministic (so
> the regression harness is reproducible), and shadow prices — "which constraint costs me
> most" is a question a plant manager actually asks.
>
> The GA scores candidates with the exact non-linear model, whole population in one batched
> predict call per generation. No surrogate gap, but stochastic and no shadow prices.

**Comment 3 — the finding I didn't want**

> The uncomfortable result: 0 of 50 optimiser blends fall inside the model's training
> support.
>
> Measured as mean distance to the 5 nearest training laydowns in standardised feature
> space, over the 99th percentile of that distance within the training set. Ratio > 1 means
> the model is extrapolating. kNN rather than Mahalanobis because origin fractions are
> bounded and variances are skewed — no distributional assumption survives here.
>
> It makes sense in hindsight: the cheapest in-band blend is by construction unlike
> anything in the history. I tried forcing blends to look historical (tighter per-bale cap)
> — it cut in-band from 0.90 to 0.82 and the saving to ~0, and lifted in-support blends
> only to 8%. Bad trade. Reverted.
>
> So the system says it out loud instead: no such blend is ever rated HIGH confidence, and
> a human must sign it off.

**Comment 4 — the gate that refused me**

> Promotion is a command: make promote APPROVER="<name>"
>
> It checks the acceptance thresholds first — not overridable, because false-accept rate is
> the hard gate: shipping out-of-spec yarn costs more than any cotton saving. Then the
> regression suite against the frozen baseline.
>
> It refused my own promotion. The saving had dropped from 6.42% to 3.02%, which the gate
> correctly reads as a regression. It only proceeded once I recorded why — a named human
> and a written reason, now sitting in the model registry next to the version.
>
> The old number came from a 15-scenario sample (11 matched). Re-run on all 50 golden
> laydowns it's 3.2%; the contamination cap costs another ~0.2. Nothing got worse. The
> measurement got honest.

**Comment 5 — code**

> Code, governance docs and the honest limitations: https://github.com/olocodewalo/governed-ml-cotton-blend-optimiser
>
> The README carries the case study, the 90-day delivery plan, the RAID register, the model
> governance policy and the measurement plan. Every number regenerates with `make data &&
> make train && make eval`, and `eval/baseline.json` is the frozen baseline the regression
> gate checks against.
>
> To be explicit: this runs on synthetic bale and laydown data from a hand-written physics
> surrogate. It demonstrates the shape of a governed ML decision system — not a validated
> result from any mill.

## Publishing checklist

1. Make the repo public and confirm CI is green.
2. `python docs/linkedin/set_links.py --repo <url>` then `python docs/linkedin/to_linkedin.py`.
3. Publish the article: title from the top of `article_linkedin.txt`, cover
   `slides/article_cover.png`, body pasted below the divider. Delete the `[H2]`/`[H3]`
   markers and apply heading styles; upload the slides where the `[IMAGE: ...]` markers say.
4. Copy the article URL, then `python docs/linkedin/set_links.py --article <url>` and
   `python docs/linkedin/set_links.py --check`.
5. Post Post A. Attach the carousel (`slides/slide_1.png` … `slide_10.png`) or lead with
   `docs/screenshots/01_review_screen.png` — that one image carries the MEDIUM confidence
   banner, the out-of-support warning and the price-age line.
6. Add alt text to every image.
7. Post the comment thread immediately, in order.
8. Reply to everything in the first two hours.
