# Fibre maturity

Maturity ratio describes how well the fibre's secondary cell wall has developed
(cellulose deposition). AFIS reports it directly; on the HVI it is entangled
with micronaire.

- Mature fibre (ratio ~0.85-0.92) is stronger, resists nep formation, and dyes
  evenly.
- **Immature fibre** is weak, kinks and tangles into **neps**, and **under-dyes**
  -- immature specks show as white flecks after dyeing.
- Immaturity comes from drought or early frost, or from picking too early.

Because micronaire = fineness x maturity, two bales with the same micronaire can
differ in maturity. Where maturity data exists, the blend is kept above a floor
(~0.80) so dyeing is predictable.
