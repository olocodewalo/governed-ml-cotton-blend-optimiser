# Micronaire variance across a blend

Even when the *average* micronaire of a laydown is on target, mixing coarse and
fine bales together (high micronaire **spread**) is harmful:

- Fibres of different fineness do not draft together smoothly, creating drafting
  waves and **thick/thin places** -> higher U% (unevenness).
- Differential dye uptake between coarse and fine fibre shows up as **barre**
  and shade variation in the fabric.
- Neps form preferentially where fine immature fibre concentrates.

Because these effects are **non-linear**, a blend with micronaire ranging
3.4-4.8 is much worse than two separate blends each held to a 0.3 window. Good
mixing practice keeps the bale-to-bale micronaire range inside ~0.4 and the
blend standard deviation low, not just the mean correct.
