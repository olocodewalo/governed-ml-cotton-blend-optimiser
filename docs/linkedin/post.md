# LinkedIn post kit

## Which format, and what each one limits

| Format | Limit | Reach | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed post | **3,000 characters** | Highest — the feed algorithm favours native posts | The hook and the headline findings |
| Carousel / document post | 300 pages; PDF or multi-image | Highest, and holds attention longest | The 10 slides |
| **Article** | **~110,000 characters**, title 100 | Much lower organic reach; found via profile and search | The full technical write-up |
| Newsletter | Same editor as an article | Article reach **plus** a notification to subscribers | Same content, if you have newsletters enabled |
| Comment | 1,250 characters each | Rides on the post | The depth, as a thread you post yourself |

**LinkedIn News is editorial — you cannot post into it.** It is written by LinkedIn's own
news editors. The five formats above are all a member can publish.

**So do both, not one.** The carousel post is what people actually see; the article is
where someone who cares reads the whole argument. Publish the article first, then post the
carousel, then drop the article link in your own first comment. (Links in the post body
get suppressed; links in comments do not.)

**Paste-ready article:** `article_linkedin.txt`, generated by
`python docs/linkedin/to_linkedin.py` from [article.md](article.md). LinkedIn's editor has
no markdown, no tables and no code blocks, so that file has the emphasis stripped, tables
flattened into readable lines, and `[H2]`/`[IMAGE: ...]` markers telling you where to apply
a heading style or upload a slide. Current size: **16,670 of 110,000 characters** — the
length is not a constraint at all.

**Article cover image:** `slides/article_cover.png` (1920×1080).

## The short-form pieces

**Hard limits:** a post is 3,000 characters, a comment is 1,250. So the depth goes in a
comment thread you post yourself.

**Non-negotiable:** the "prototype on synthetic data" line stays in the post body, not
only in the comments. Every number comes from `eval/baseline.json` (model v2, 50 held-out
laydowns). Run `python docs/linkedin/check.py` to verify each block still fits.

## Post A — main post

> My first write-up claimed a ~6% cost saving.
> I audited my own project and it fell to 3.0%.
>
> That audit is the part I'm proud of. Here's the whole thing.
>
> **The decision.** In a ring-spinning mill, raw cotton is 55-65% of yarn cost. Every week
> a mixing master picks the bale laydown — which bales, in what proportion — trading ₹/kg
> against CSP, U%, imperfections and ends-down. In most mills that sits in one expert's
> head and a spreadsheet, isn't re-optimised when cotton prices move, and leaves no record
> of why a blend was chosen. When that person retires, the reasoning leaves with them.
>
> **Why ML, not a rule.** Yarn quality is non-linear in the blend. Short-fibre content
> penalises strength with an exponent above 1. Micronaire *variance* hurts evenness
> independently of the mean — two blends with identical average micronaire spin
> differently. Neps come from immature fibre and short fibre interacting. A linear cost
> rule sees none of it.
>
> **What I built, and why each piece:**
>
> → **LightGBM** for CSP / U% / imperfections / ends-down. 400 laydowns, 15 tabular
> features, strong interactions — the regime where GBMs beat both linear models and neural
> nets. Native quantile objective and SHAP, both of which I needed.
>
> → **Conformalized quantile regression** for the P10-P90 bands. Raw quantile heads on 400
> rows overfit and come out too narrow — and a narrow-but-wrong band is worse than no band,
> because the auto-suggest gate keys on the P10.
>
> → **MILP (PuLP/CBC)** for the optimiser: fast, deterministic, gives shadow prices. It
> can't use the real model — quality depends on weighted *variances*, quadratic in the
> weights — so it optimises a linear surrogate, then the full model re-scores and margins
> inflate if the blend fell out of band. Plus a **DEAP genetic algorithm** that optimises
> against the exact non-linear model, whole population scored in one batched predict call.
>
> → **RAG over 18 fibre-science notes + Claude** for the rationale. Advisory only. It never
> computes a number, and falls back to a deterministic template with no API key. This is
> not a generative-AI use case and pretending otherwise would be the tell.
>
> → **Streamlit** review screen, **SQLite** audit log, JSON model registry, **GitHub
> Actions** CI.
>
> **Then I audited it against my own governance doc, and found five gaps** — including an
> unapproved model quietly serving predictions, and an optimiser that had put 54.6% of a
> blend into the one origin a mill caps for contamination risk. Details in the comments.
>
> Measured properly on all 50 held-out laydowns: 3.0% saving at strictly matched quality
> (95% CI 0.8-5.5%), in-band 90%, false-accept 2%.
>
> A smaller number I can defend beats a bigger one I can't.
>
> Prototype on synthetic data — the point is the governance, not the number.
>
> If you run blending in a mill: what would you need to see before letting a system like
> this influence a real laydown?

## Comment thread — post these yourself, in order, right after publishing

**Comment 1 — the five gaps**

> The audit, in full. My governance doc described controls my code did not have:
>
> 1. The model failed its own policy. I required 70-90% prediction-interval coverage;
> imperfections and ends-down sat at 67% and 68%.
> 2. An unapproved model was serving — training silently overwrote the "current" pointer,
> so a candidate went live without sign-off.
> 3. Drift detection, an out-of-support check, a price-freshness guard and a contamination
> cap were all written in the policy. None existed in code.
> 4. The optimiser found that gap: on a 20s Ne scenario it put 54.6% of the blend into the
> origin a mixing master caps for contamination risk, because it was cheap.
> 5. No CI — while the policy said the regression suite "fails CI".
>
> This is the ordinary failure mode of ML governance: the document is the control, and
> nothing enforces it.

**Comment 2 — the uncertainty fix, in detail**

> On the intervals, since that's the part with real teeth:
>
> LightGBM quantile heads trained on ~400 rows overfit in-sample, so the P10-P90 band comes
> out too narrow. My first attempt scaled the band by a calibration factor and still
> under-covered.
>
> The fix was 5-fold cross-conformalized quantile regression: train the quantile heads
> out-of-fold, compute conformity scores max(lo − y, y − hi), take the finite-sample
> corrected quantile, and widen additively. Under exchangeability that gives a
> distribution-free coverage guarantee.
>
> Coverage went to 0.76 / 0.80, inside the policy band — with no change to point accuracy.
> The predictions were never the problem; the honesty about them was.

**Comment 3 — the finding I didn't want**

> The uncomfortable result: 0 of 50 optimiser blends fall inside the model's training
> support.
>
> Measured as mean distance to the 5 nearest training laydowns in standardised feature
> space, over the 99th percentile of that distance within the training set. Ratio > 1 means
> the model is extrapolating. kNN rather than Mahalanobis because origin fractions are
> bounded and variances are skewed — no distributional assumption survives here.
>
> It makes sense in hindsight: the cheapest in-band blend is by construction unlike
> anything in the history. I tried forcing blends to look historical (tighter per-bale cap)
> — it cut in-band from 0.90 to 0.82 and the saving to ~0, and lifted in-support blends
> only to 8%. Bad trade. Reverted.
>
> So the system says it out loud instead: no such blend is ever rated HIGH confidence, and
> a human must sign it off.

**Comment 4 — the gate that refused me**

> Promotion is a command: make promote APPROVER="<name>"
>
> It checks the acceptance thresholds first — not overridable, because false-accept rate is
> the hard gate: shipping out-of-spec yarn costs more than any cotton saving. Then the
> regression suite against the frozen baseline.
>
> It refused my own promotion. The saving had dropped from 6.42% to 3.02%, which the gate
> correctly reads as a regression. It only proceeded once I recorded why — a named human
> and a written reason, now sitting in the model registry next to the version.
>
> The old number came from a 15-scenario sample (11 matched). Re-run on all 50 golden
> laydowns it's 3.2%; the contamination cap costs another ~0.2. Nothing got worse. The
> measurement got honest.

**Comment 5 — links**

> Repo: https://github.com/<your-username>/cotton-blend-optimisation
> Full technical write-up (every choice and why): <LinkedIn article URL or repo link>
>
> The README carries the case study, 90-day delivery plan, RAID register, model governance
> policy and measurement plan. Numbers regenerate with `make data && make train &&
> make eval`.
>
> To be explicit: this runs on synthetic bale and laydown data from a hand-written physics
> surrogate. It demonstrates the shape of a governed ML decision system — not a validated
> result from any mill.

## Posting checklist

1. Make the repo public and confirm CI is green **before** posting.
2. Upload `slides/slide_1.png` … `slide_10.png` in order — as a multi-image carousel, or
   combined into one PDF for a document post (document posts hold attention longer).
3. Alternative opener: `docs/screenshots/01_review_screen.png` — the MEDIUM confidence
   banner, out-of-support warning and price-age line tell the whole story in one image.
4. Add alt text to every image.
5. Links go in the **comments**, never the post body.
6. Post the comment thread immediately, in order, so the depth is there before readers
   arrive.
7. Reply to everything in the first two hours.
