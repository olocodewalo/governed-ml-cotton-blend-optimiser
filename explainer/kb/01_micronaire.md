# Micronaire

Micronaire is a combined measure of fibre fineness and maturity, obtained by
measuring the airflow resistance of a fixed mass of cotton. Typical upland
cotton runs 3.5-4.9.

- **Low micronaire (< 3.5)**: fine but often immature fibre. More fibres per
  cross-section (good for strength and fine counts) but immature fibre tangles
  easily, raising **neps** and dye-uptake variation.
- **High micronaire (> 4.9)**: coarse, mature fibre. Fewer fibres in the yarn
  cross-section, so yarn strength and evenness suffer at fine counts; also
  causes a harsher handle.
- The **ideal blend micronaire is roughly 4.0** for most ring-spun counts.

Micronaire is the property most tightly watched in **long-term blending**
because small drifts move yarn count and dye lots.
