# Short-fibre content (SFC)

SFC is the percentage of fibres shorter than 12.7 mm (by weight or by number,
AFIS). It is arguably the most damaging single property and the hardest to see
on a basic HVI report.

Effects, all **accelerating (non-linear)** as SFC rises:

- Short fibres do not get twisted in fully; they migrate to the yarn surface ->
  **hairiness**, pilling, and lower strength.
- They cause drafting irregularity -> higher **U%** and **imperfections**
  (neps, thick/thin places).
- They increase **ends-down** and flics/fly at the machine.
- They inflate spinning **waste** at the comber and card.

Because the penalty curve steepens, moving a blend from 13% to 11% SFC helps far
more than moving it from 9% to 7%. Combing removes short fibre but at a yield
and cost penalty. SFC rises with mechanical handling, low micronaire, and
drought-stressed crops.
