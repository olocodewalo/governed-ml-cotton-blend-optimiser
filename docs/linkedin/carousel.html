The decision

Raw cotton is 55–65% of yarn cost.
One person decides it weekly.

A mixing master picks which bales go into the laydown and in what proportions, trading ₹/kg against spun-yarn quality. Today that lives in one expert's head and a spreadsheet, is not re-optimised when prices move, and leaves no record of why a blend was chosen.
  • Quality is non-linear in the blend. Short-fibre content penalises strength with a power > 1. Micronaire variance hurts evenness independently of the mean. A linear cost rule cannot see either.
  • So the ML earns its place — it predicts CSP, U%, imperfections and ends-down from the blend's aggregate fibre profile.
  • The optimiser treats quality as a constraint, not an objective: cheapest blend that still meets the count's spec band and stays near the rolling 30-laydown average.

Architecture

The LLM is nowhere near
the decision.

Data layer
2,000 synthetic bale tests + 500 historical laydowns with actual yarn QC. One feature function shared by training and serving.
ML quality model
LightGBM multi-output + P10/P90 heads, calibrated with 5-fold conformalized quantile regression.
▼
Cost optimiser
MILP (PuLP/CBC) on a linear surrogate + GA (DEAP) on the full model. Guards run first: stale prices refused, contamination-prone origins hard-capped.
Confidence policy
HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW. Out-of-support blends are never HIGH, so a human must review them.
▼
RAG + LLM explainer
18 fibre-science notes retrieved, Claude writes a 4–6 sentence rationale. Advisory only, never touches a number, falls back to a template with no API key.
Human + audit
Approve / Adjust / Reject. Every decision, confidence and reason written to SQLite.
No agents in the decision path. The weekly blend has to be deterministic, reproducible and auditable — the whole governance layer depends on that.

The audit

My docs described controls
my code did not have.

  • The model failed its own policy. The policy required 70–90% interval coverage. Imperfections and ends-down were at 67% and 68%.
  • An unapproved model was serving. Training overwrote the "current" pointer, so a candidate went live without sign-off.
  • Written but never built: drift detection, an out-of-support check, a price-freshness guard, a contamination cap. All four were in the governance doc. None were in the code.
  • The optimiser exploited that. On a 20s scenario it put 54.6% of the blend into the one origin a mixing master caps for contamination risk.
  • CI did not exist — while the policy said the regression suite "fails CI".
This is the ordinary failure mode of ML governance: the document is the control, and nothing enforces it.

That last row is the uncomfortable one. The cheapest in-band blend is, by construction, unlike anything in the training data. I tried forcing blends to look historical; it cost the saving and the in-band rate and fixed nothing. What it takes to ship

The gate that refused me.

$ make promote APPROVER="Ankit Nandkishor Agrawal" decision.false_accept_rate 0.020 <= 0.05 ok quality.ends_down.pi_coverage 0.800 in [0.7,0.9] ok optimiser.in_band_rate 0.900 >= 0.9 ok regression vs baseline v1: saving_at_matched_quality 6.420 -> 3.020 REGRESSED REFUSED: fix them or record a CAB decision with --accept-regression "<reason>"
It took a recorded reason, signed by a named human, before the model was allowed to serve. That record now sits in the registry next to the model version — which is the point.
